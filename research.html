Research
Our group focuses on the development of AI-driven computational methods and their integration with experimental validation for biological molecule design and discovery. We are interested in how machine learning, structural modeling, and wet-lab feedback can be combined to improve both predictive accuracy and practical usefulness in real research settings.
Computational Protein Design

We develop computational strategies for protein design, including sequence optimization, structure-aware modeling, and property prediction. Our goal is to generate proteins with desired stability, activity, specificity, and binding behavior, while keeping the workflow compatible with experimental validation.

AI-based Methodology Development

We build machine learning methods for molecular and protein engineering, with an emphasis on generalization, interpretability, and data efficiency. These methods are designed to support scientific discovery rather than function only as standalone prediction tools.

Closed-loop Wet-Lab Validation

Computational predictions are validated through experimental assays to measure protein function, binding, expression, and other biochemical properties. This closed-loop workflow helps refine models and improves the reliability of design and discovery.

Applications in Biological Discovery

We apply our approaches to problems in enzyme engineering, therapeutic design, and protein function understanding. By combining computation and experiment, we aim to accelerate the discovery of useful biological systems.

Typical directions in the lab include:
  • Protein sequence and structure design
  • Modeling protein stability and function
  • Data-driven methodology for molecular engineering
  • Integration of prediction models with experimental assays
